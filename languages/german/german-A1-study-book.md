# Chapter 1 — The Alphabet & Pronunciation {#chapter-1}

## 1.1 The German Alphabet

German uses the same 26 letters as English, plus **4 special characters**:

| Special character | Name | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **ä** | A-Umlaut | Like the "e" in "bed" |
| **ö** | O-Umlaut | Like the French "eu" in "feu" — round your lips as if to say "o" but say "e" |
| **ü** | U-Umlaut | Like the French "u" — round your lips as if to say "oo" but say "ee" |
| **ß** | Eszett / scharfes S | Like a double "ss" — e.g. *Straße* (street) |

## 1.2 Key Pronunciation Rules

German pronunciation is very consistent — once you learn the rules, you can read almost any word correctly.

| Letter / combo | Pronunciation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **w** | like English "v" | *Wasser* (water) = "Vasser" |
| **v** | like English "f" | *Vater* (father) = "Fater" |
| **z** | like "ts" | *Zeit* (time) = "Tseit" |
| **sp / st** at start of word | "shp" / "sht" | *Sprache* = "Shprache", *Straße* = "Shtrasse" |
| **ei** | like English "eye" | *Wein* (wine) = "Vine" |
| **ie** | like English "ee" | *viel* (much) = "feel" |
| **eu / äu** | like "oy" | *neu* (new) = "noy" |
| **ch** after a/o/u | guttural sound (like Scottish "loch") | *Bach* |
| **ch** after e/i | soft, like "sh" but breathier | *ich* |
| **r** | rolled or guttural at back of throat | *rot* (red) |
| **-ig** at end of word | like "-ich" | *richtig* = "richtich" |

## 1.3 Capital Letters

In German, **all nouns are capitalised** — not just proper nouns.

> *Das ist ein **H**und.* (That is a dog.)
> *Ich trinke **W**asser.* (I drink water.)

**Key phrases:**

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *Ich heiße Anna.* | My name is Anna. |
| *Ich bin Anna.* | I am Anna. |
| *Wie heißen Sie?* | What is your name? (formal) |

# Chapter 3 — Articles & Noun Genders (der, die, das) {#chapter-3}

## 3.1 The Three Genders

Every German noun has a **grammatical gender**: masculine, feminine, or neuter. This is one of the most important things to learn — and you must memorise the gender with every new noun.

| Gender | Definite article ("the") | Indefinite article ("a/an") |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | **der** | **ein** |
| Feminine | **die** | **eine** |
| Neuter | **das** | **ein** |
| Plural (all genders) | **die** | **—** |

**Examples:**

| German | Article | English |
|---|---|---|
| *der Mann* | masculine | the man |
| *die Frau* | feminine | the woman |
| *das Kind* | neuter | the child |
| *die Männer* | plural | the men |

## 3.2 Tips for Guessing Gender

There are no absolute rules, but these patterns help:

**Usually masculine (der):**
- Male people & animals: *der Vater* (father), *der Hund* (dog)
- Days, months, seasons: *der Montag*, *der Januar*, *der Sommer*
- Words ending in **-er, -en, -el**: *der Lehrer* (teacher), *der Wagen* (car)

**Usually feminine (die):**
- Female people & animals: *die Mutter* (mother), *die Katze* (cat)
- Words ending in **-ung, -heit, -keit, -ion, -schaft**: *die Wohnung* (apartment), *die Freiheit* (freedom)
- Numbers: *die Eins, die Zwei*

**Usually neuter (das):**
- Diminutives ending in **-chen, -lein**: *das Mädchen* (girl), *das Kindlein*
- Infinitives used as nouns: *das Essen* (the eating/food)
- Most metals and chemical elements

> ⚠️ **Golden rule:** Never learn a noun without its article. Don't say "Tisch" — say "**der** Tisch".

## 3.3 Negation with "kein"

To say "no" or "not a", use **kein/keine/kein** (same pattern as ein/eine/ein):

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| | *kein Mann* | *keine Frau* | *kein Kind* |
| Plural | | *keine Kinder* | |

> *Ich habe **keinen** Hund.* → I have no dog. (accusative — see Chapter 8)
> *Das ist **keine** Katze.* → That is not a cat.

# Chapter 4 — Numbers, Dates & Time {#chapter-4}

## 4.1 Numbers 0–100

**0–20:**
0 null | 1 eins | 2 zwei | 3 drei | 4 vier | 5 fünf | 6 sechs | 7 sieben | 8 acht | 9 neun | 10 zehn
11 elf | 12 zwölf | 13 dreizehn | 14 vierzehn | 15 fünfzehn | 16 sechzehn | 17 siebzehn | 18 achtzehn | 19 neunzehn | 20 zwanzig

**Tens:**
20 zwanzig | 30 dreißig | 40 vierzig | 50 fünfzig | 60 sechzig | 70 siebzig | 80 achtzig | 90 neunzig | 100 hundert

**21–99 pattern:** Say the unit first, then "und", then the ten.
> 21 = *einundzwanzig* | 35 = *fünfunddreißig* | 99 = *neunundneunzig*

## 4.2 Days, Months & Seasons

**Days of the week (all masculine — der):**
Montag | Dienstag | Mittwoch | Donnerstag | Freitag | Samstag | Sonntag

**Months (all masculine — der):**
Januar | Februar | März | April | Mai | Juni | Juli | August | September | Oktober | November | Dezember

**Seasons (all masculine — der):**
Frühling (spring) | Sommer (summer) | Herbst (autumn) | Winter (winter)

**Useful date phrases:**
> *Heute ist Montag, der 5. März.* — Today is Monday, the 5th of March.
> *Ich bin am 12. Juni geboren.* — I was born on 12 June.
> *Wann haben Sie Geburtstag?* — When is your birthday?

## 4.3 Telling the Time

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *Es ist zwei Uhr.* | It is 2 o'clock. |
| *Es ist halb drei.* | It is half past two. (lit. "half three") |
| *Es ist Viertel nach zwei.* | It is quarter past two. |
| *Es ist Viertel vor drei.* | It is quarter to three. |
| *Es ist zwanzig nach zwei.* | It is twenty past two. |
| *Es ist zehn vor drei.* | It is ten to three. |

> ⚠️ **Watch out!** *halb drei* = 2:30 (half of three = halfway to three), NOT 3:30!

**Asking the time:**
> *Wie viel Uhr ist es?* / *Wie spät ist es?* — What time is it?

# Chapter 5 — Present Tense Verbs (Präsens) {#chapter-5}

## 5.1 Regular Verb Conjugation

German verbs in the infinitive end in **-en** (or sometimes -n). To conjugate, remove -en and add the correct ending.

**Example: *spielen* (to play) — stem: *spiel-***

| Pronoun | Ending | Form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| ich | -e | *spiele* | I play |
| du | -st | *spielst* | you play (informal) |
| er/sie/es | -t | *spielt* | he/she/it plays |
| wir | -en | *spielen* | we play |
| ihr | -t | *spielt* | you all play |
| Sie/sie | -en | *spielen* | you (formal) / they play |

**More regular verbs to practise:**

| Infinitive | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *wohnen* | to live |
| *lernen* | to learn |
| *kommen* | to come |
| *kaufen* | to buy |
| *arbeiten* | to work |
| *heißen* | to be called |

> Note: *arbeiten* adds an extra "e": *du arbeitest*, *er arbeitet*

## 5.2 Common Irregular Verbs

Some verbs change their stem vowel in the **du** and **er/sie/es** forms:

| Infinitive | ich | du | er/sie/es | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *fahren* | fahre | **fährst** | **fährt** | to drive/travel |
| *lesen* | lese | **liest** | **liest** | to read |
| *sehen* | sehe | **siehst** | **sieht** | to see |
| *sprechen* | spreche | **sprichst** | **spricht** | to speak |
| *essen* | esse | **isst** | **isst** | to eat |
| *nehmen* | nehme | **nimmst** | **nimmt** | to take |

## 5.3 Word Order

In a standard German sentence: **Subject → Verb → Rest**

> *Ich **lerne** jeden Tag Deutsch.* (I learn German every day.)

When something other than the subject comes first, the verb stays in **position 2** (verb-second rule):

> *Jeden Tag **lerne** ich Deutsch.* (Every day I learn German.)

The verb always occupies the second position, regardless of what comes first.

# Chapter 6 — Personal Pronouns & the Verb "sein" {#chapter-6}

## 6.1 Personal Pronouns

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *ich* | I |
| *du* | you (informal singular) |
| *er* | he |
| *sie* | she |
| *es* | it |
| *wir* | we |
| *ihr* | you (informal plural) |
| *Sie* | you (formal singular & plural) |
| *sie* | they |

> **Tip:** *sie* (lowercase) = she or they. *Sie* (uppercase) = formal you. Context and verb form tell you which is which.

## 6.2 The Verb "sein" (to be)

*Sein* is highly irregular — memorise these forms completely:

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ich | **bin** | I am |
| du | **bist** | you are |
| er/sie/es | **ist** | he/she/it is |
| wir | **sind** | we are |
| ihr | **seid** | you all are |
| Sie/sie | **sind** | you (formal) / they are |

**Using sein:**
> *Ich **bin** Arzt.* — I am a doctor. (no article with professions!)
> *Du **bist** sehr nett.* — You are very nice.
> *Er **ist** aus Österreich.* — He is from Austria.
> *Wir **sind** Studenten.* — We are students.

## 6.3 The Verb "haben" (to have)

Also irregular and very common:

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ich | **habe** | I have |
| du | **hast** | you have |
| er/sie/es | **hat** | he/she/it has |
| wir | **haben** | we have |
| ihr | **habt** | you all have |
| Sie/sie | **haben** | you (formal) / they have |

> *Ich **habe** einen Bruder.* — I have a brother.
> *Hast du Zeit?* — Do you have time?
> *Sie **hat** eine Katze.* — She has a cat.

# Chapter 8 — The Accusative Case {#chapter-8}

## 8.1 What Is the Accusative Case?

In German, nouns change their article depending on their role in the sentence. The **accusative case** is used for the **direct object** — the thing being acted upon.

> *Ich kaufe **den Apfel**.* — I buy the apple. ("the apple" is being bought)

**Nominative vs. Accusative articles:**

| Gender | Nominative ("subject") | Accusative ("direct object") |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | **der** / ein | **den** / einen |
| Feminine | die / eine | die / eine *(no change)* |
| Neuter | das / ein | das / ein *(no change)* |
| Plural | die / — | die / — *(no change)* |

> ⚠️ **Only masculine articles change** in the accusative (der → den, ein → einen, kein → keinen)!

## 8.2 Examples

> *Ich sehe **der** Mann.* ❌
> *Ich sehe **den** Mann.* ✅ (masculine → accusative)

> *Ich kaufe **die** Tasche.* ✅ (feminine, no change)

> *Er hat **ein** Auto.* ✅ (neuter, no change)

> *Sie liebt **ihren** Bruder.* ✅ (masculine possessive, accusative)

## 8.3 Common Verbs That Take the Accusative

Most action verbs are followed by accusative objects:

*haben* (to have), *sehen* (to see), *kaufen* (to buy), *essen* (to eat), *trinken* (to drink), *brauchen* (to need), *mögen* (to like), *lieben* (to love)

# Chapter 10 — Modal Verbs (können, müssen, wollen) {#chapter-10}

## 10.1 What Are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs express **ability, necessity, desire, or permission**. They are used with a second verb (infinitive) which goes to the **end of the sentence**.

> *Ich **kann** Deutsch **sprechen**.* — I can speak German.
> *Du **musst** jetzt **gehen**.* — You must go now.

## 10.2 The Main Modal Verbs

| Infinitive | Meaning | ich | du | er/sie/es | wir / Sie / sie | ihr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *können* | can / to be able to | kann | kannst | kann | können | könnt |
| *müssen* | must / to have to | muss | musst | muss | müssen | müsst |
| *wollen* | to want to | will | willst | will | wollen | wollt |
| *dürfen* | may / to be allowed to | darf | darfst | darf | dürfen | dürft |
| *sollen* | should / to be supposed to | soll | sollst | soll | sollen | sollt |
| *möchten* | would like to | möchte | möchtest | möchte | möchten | möchtet |

## 10.3 Word Order with Modals

The modal verb takes **position 2**, and the infinitive goes to the **end**.

> *Ich **möchte** einen Kaffee **haben**.* — I would like to have a coffee.
> *Sie **muss** morgen **arbeiten**.* — She has to work tomorrow.
> *Wir **können** nicht **kommen**.* — We cannot come.

In **questions**, the modal comes first:
> ***Kannst** du mir **helfen**?* — Can you help me?
> ***Darf** ich **rauchen**?* — May I smoke?

# Chapter 11 — The Dative Case & Prepositions {#chapter-11}

## 11.1 What Is the Dative Case?

The **dative case** marks the **indirect object** — the person or thing that receives the action.

> *Ich gebe **dem Mann** das Buch.* — I give the man the book.
> (the book = accusative; the man = dative)

**Dative articles:**

| Gender | Nominative | Dative |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | der / ein | **dem / einem** |
| Feminine | die / eine | **der / einer** |
| Neuter | das / ein | **dem / einem** |
| Plural | die / — | **den + n** |

> *Ich helfe **der** Frau.* (feminine, dative)
> *Sie dankt **dem** Lehrer.* (masculine, dative)

## 11.2 Verbs That Use Dative

These verbs are always followed by the dative:

*helfen* (to help), *danken* (to thank), *gehören* (to belong to), *antworten* (to answer), *gefallen* (to please/like), *glauben* (to believe)

> *Das gefällt **mir**.* — I like that. (lit: That pleases me.)
> *Das Buch gehört **meinem** Bruder.* — The book belongs to my brother.

## 11.3 Common Prepositions + Dative

These prepositions **always** take the dative:

**aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu, gegenüber**

| German | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *mit* | with | *Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus.* |
| *bei* | at / near / with (at someone's place) | *Ich bin **bei meiner** Freundin.* |
| *nach* | to (cities/countries), after | *Wir fahren **nach** Berlin.* |
| *von* | from / by | *Das ist ein Brief **von meinem** Vater.* |
| *zu* | to (people, places) | *Ich gehe **zu meiner** Mutter.* |
| *aus* | from (origin) / out of | *Ich komme **aus der** Schweiz.* |
| *seit* | since / for (time) | *Ich lerne Deutsch **seit** drei Monaten.* |

## 11.4 Two-Way Prepositions

These prepositions take **accusative for movement** and **dative for location**:

*an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen*

> *Ich gehe **in die** Schule.* (movement → accusative)
> *Ich bin **in der** Schule.* (location → dative)
> *Das Buch liegt **auf dem** Tisch.* (location → dative)
> *Ich lege das Buch **auf den** Tisch.* (movement → accusative)

**Quick trick:** Ask yourself — is something **moving to** a place (accusative) or **already at** a place (dative)?

# Chapter 12 — Simple Past (war & hatte) and Review {#chapter-12}

## 12.1 The Simple Past of "sein" (war)

In everyday speech, the simple past of *sein* is used even in conversations (unlike most other verbs which use Perfekt — see A2 guide):

| Pronoun | sein (to be) → war |
|---|---|
| ich | **war** |
| du | **warst** |
| er/sie/es | **war** |
| wir | **waren** |
| ihr | **wart** |
| Sie/sie | **waren** |

> *Ich **war** gestern in Berlin.* — I was in Berlin yesterday.
> *Das Wetter **war** schön.* — The weather was nice.

## 12.2 The Simple Past of "haben" (hatte)

| Pronoun | haben (to have) → hatte |
|---|---|
| ich | **hatte** |
| du | **hattest** |
| er/sie/es | **hatte** |
| wir | **hatten** |
| ihr | **hattet** |
| Sie/sie | **hatten** |

> *Ich **hatte** keine Zeit.* — I had no time.
> *Wir **hatten** Spaß.* — We had fun.
